Findings and limits

  1. Authentication is not trust. All 25 HTML-smuggling emails came from a compromised real vendor and passed SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Only the attachment analysis caught them.
  2. BEC has no payload. Gift-card and payroll scams carry no link or file. They were caught by sender identity (executive name on Gmail, lookalike employee domain, Reply-To mismatch) plus a financial request under pressure.
  3. OAuth consent phishing uses the real Microsoft login page. The domain is genuine; the danger is in the requested scopes and the outside redirect URL.
  4. Keyword rules punish normal business mail. Every legitimate invoice and every mailing-list post was flagged, while 81% of real phishing got through.
  5. Limit: delivery-fee lures with short links score Suspicious, not Phishing. Closing that gap needs URL reputation or sandbox detonation, which this offline engine does not do.

About the data. 400 synthetic emails for a fictional company, Harborline Freight: 240 legitimate (including invoices, newsletters with tracking links, mailing lists that break DMARC, and urgent-sounding real notices) and 160 phishing across 8 techniques. Attacker domains use reserved .test and .example names (RFC 2606). Results are measured on this set; real mail is more varied.
